# Literature notes: global stability of x' = -x + J tanh(x), J = (g/sqrt N) G, routes past g = 1/sqrt2
(compiled 2026-11-26; "READ" = read full-text passages; "ABS" = abstract/metadata only; "KNOWN" = from background knowledge, not re-verified this session)

## Q2. Random rate networks: rigorous results

### Touboul, "Universality in randomly connected neural networks: old and new on a conjecture of Geman and Hwang", arXiv:2610.38893 (Oct 2026). READ (passages)
- Proves: (weakly heterogeneous, Var o(1/n)) quenched convergence to McKean-Vlasov on every finite time interval under 4th moment; (strongly heterogeneous, Var ~ 1/n, our regime) convergence of the network to the DMFT/self-consistent Gaussian-process limit, AVERAGED over sub-Gaussian couplings, on every FINITE time interval [0,T], via relative-entropy comparison to Gaussian couplings; heavy-tailed alpha-stable conjecture, existence/uniqueness of limit.
- Nothing on long-time behaviour / global stability / convergence to 0 for g<1. All results are finite-horizon. It explicitly notes rare large couplings "may affect long-time stability and the transition to chaos" (heavy tails) — i.e., long-time is outside scope.
- Useful review pointers: Geman-Hwang (linear S, universality), Ben Arous-Guionnet 1995/1997, Cabana-Touboul 2013/2018 (LDP for random neural nets with Gaussian couplings, finite T), Celentano-Cheng-Montanari arXiv:2112.07572.

### Sherf, Tang, Hafner, Touboul, Pitkow, Bassler, Josic, "Complexity and dynamics of partially symmetric random neural networks", arXiv:2512.24439 (Dec 2025). READ (appendices A, B)
- Model x' = -x + W S(x), elliptic ensemble with reciprocity tau, g_eff = g(1+tau).
- Claims "when g_eff<1 the system is contracting as N->inf and x*=0 is the unique fixed point". The argument is NOT a rigorous global-stability proof:
  * App. A: rightmost eigenvalue of W*Delta(d) <= g(1+tau) is derived from the deterministic-equivalent / inhomogeneous-elliptic-law equations of ref [21] (Kuczala-Sharpee-type), which hold for a FIXED profile d independent of W, not uniformly over d chosen adversarially (d = S'(x*) depends on W).
  * App. B: Brouwer degree argument: IF every fixed point has Hurwitz Jacobian, then the fixed point is unique (degree = (-1)^N). This is uniqueness of equilibria, not global attractivity (does not exclude limit cycles/chaos).
- Takeaway: at most heuristic evidence for g<1 uniqueness; the degree trick is a reusable lemma: "Hurwitz at every equilibrium + homotopy to (g_eff-1)x  =>  unique equilibrium".

## Q1. Absolute stability / diagonal stability: exact statements

### Hopfield-type ABST, x' = -Dx + T g(x) + I  (D>0 diagonal; ABST = unique GAS equilibrium for EVERY g in class and EVERY input I)
- Forti, Manetti, Marini, "Necessary and sufficient condition for absolute stability of neural networks", IEEE TCAS-I 41(7):491-494, 1994. KNOWN + confirmed second-hand (Liang, "A simple proof of a NS condition for absolute stability of symmetric neural networks", IEEE TCAS-I 1997/98, https://www.ece.lsu.edu/liang/publications/casi97-1.pdf, READ): class S = bounded C^1 sigmoids with g'>0 and ARBITRARY gain; for SYMMETRIC T, ABST <=> T negative semidefinite (equivalently -T in P0 for symmetric T). For general T, -T in P0 is necessary for existence/uniqueness of equilibrium for all g,I (Forti-Manetti-Marini; Forti 1994 JDE) but not sufficient for ABST.
- Kaszkurewicz & Bhaya, "On a class of globally stable neural circuits", IEEE TCAS-I 41(2):171-174, 1994 (KNOWN): T in LDSS (exists Lambda>0 diag with Lambda T + T^T Lambda <= 0) => ABST w.r.t. S. Liang's letter (READ) extends to locally Lipschitz nondecreasing g, finite or infinite sector.
- Forti & Tesi, "New conditions for global stability of neural networks with application to linear and quadratic programming problems", IEEE TCAS-I 42(7):354-366, 1995 (KNOWN): -T LDS (or LDSS + extra) => ABST incl. finite-sector/Lipschitz activations. Finite-gain version (activation slope <= G): LDS of T - D G^{-1} suffices (Liang & Si, IEEE TNN 2001; Hu & Wang 2002) — this is our "J - I Lyapunov diagonally stable" condition with D=G=I.
- Chu, Zhang, Zhang, "Necessary and sufficient condition for absolute stability of normal neural networks", Neural Networks 16:1223, 2003, doi:10.1016/S0893-6080(03)00075-3 (ABS): for NORMAL T, ABST <=> Re lambda(T) <= 0. Chu & Zhang, Neural Networks 19:1347 (2006), doi:10.1016/j.neunet.2006.06.003 (ABS): NS condition under a solvable-Lie-algebra hypothesis on T (generalizes symmetric/normal).
- IMPORTANT for us: all these ABST results allow ARBITRARY (unbounded) gain / infinite sector, so the conditions are on T alone and are scale-invariant. None is a necessity theorem for the finite sector [0,1] (gain <= 1) class with T = J, i.e. none says "LDS of J - I is necessary". For non-normal, non-symmetric T, no NS characterization of ABST is known to me; NS results exist only for symmetric, normal, Lie-algebraic-solvable T. Random iid J is none of these.

### Diagonal stability: characterizations (useful for the 1/sqrt2 obstruction and its dual)
- Barker, Berman, Plemmons, "Positive diagonal solutions to the Lyapunov equations", Lin. Multilin. Alg. 5:249-256, 1978 (KNOWN; restated in arXiv:1907.07089 and Mason's note, READ second-hand): A is LDS iff for every nonzero PSD X, AX has at least one negative diagonal entry. [Dual certificate of non-LDS = PSD X with diag(AX) >= 0.]
- Kraaijevanger (1991): A LDS iff A∘S is a P-matrix for all symmetric S >= 0 entrywise with unit diagonal.
- Cross 1978: LDS => (multiplicative and additive) D-stable; LDS matrices are P-matrices (−A); inclusions proper for n>=3.
- Arcak & Sontag, Automatica 42:1531 (2006) and Arcak, arXiv:math/0504275 (READ): for the cyclic negative-feedback matrix class, LDS <=> secant condition gamma_1...gamma_n < sec(pi/n)^n; necessity there comes because failing secant => A not Hurwitz. This is the one class where LDS is NS for stability; irrelevant structurally to iid J.
- Kaszkurewicz & Bhaya, "Robust stability and diagonal Liapunov functions", SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 14(2):508-520, 1993, doi:10.1137/0614036 (ABS only — full text not retrievable): abstract says diagonal Lyapunov functions are "essential" (necessary) for robust stability of linear systems under a broad class of perturbations, generalizing Persidskii. I could NOT verify the exact perturbation class. My (unverified) recollection: it is a class of diagonal, state- and time-dependent infinite-sector perturbations x' = A D(x,t) x — NOT the [0,1]-sector form x' = (-I + J D)x. Worth checking the book (Kaszkurewicz-Bhaya 2000, Ch. 2-3) before relying on it.

### Lyapunov-diagonal failure => destabilizing sector nonlinearity? (dual LMIs)
- Gyotoku, Yuno, Ebihara, Magron, Peaucelle, Tarbouriech, "On dual of LMIs for absolute stability analysis of nonlinear feedback systems with static O'Shea-Zames-Falb multipliers", arXiv:2411.14339 (2024); discrete-time companion arXiv:2503.05875 (2025). READ.
  * Primal LMI (slope [0,1], repeated? no — general diagonal Phi): find P, M with M in Z^m (nonpositive off-diagonals), M1 >= 0, 1^T M >= 0 (doubly hyperdominant), s.t. KYP-type LMI < 0. Dual: H PSD etc.
  * Thm 1/2: if the dual LMI has a RANK-ONE solution H = [h1;h2][h1;h2]^T, then there is an explicit (odd, for Thm 2) slope-[0,1] piecewise-linear phi_wc such that Phi_wc = diag(phi_wc,...,phi_wc) (a REPEATED nonlinearity!) makes x(0)=h1 an equilibrium, so the system is not absolutely stable. Higher rank: inconclusive.
- Specialization to our case (my own derivation, check): static diagonal multiplier for sector [0,1] at frequency 0 is exactly LDS of J - I. A rank-one Barker-Berman-Plemmons certificate X = vv^T with v_i((J-I)v)_i >= 0 for all i gives D = diag(v_i/(Jv)_i) in [0,1]^N with (I - JD)(Jv) = 0, i.e. a NONZERO EQUILIBRIUM for the linear sector-[0,1] gain phi_i(x) = d_i x. So: if the non-LDS certificate for g in (1/sqrt2, 1) can be taken rank one, absolute stability over the sector class genuinely fails and any proof must use tanh-specific structure. Conversely if max_{D in [0,1]^N} spectral abscissa(JD) < 1 w.h.p., the certificate is necessarily high rank. Diagnostic worth computing numerically: s(g,N) = max_{D in [0,1]^N} Re lambda_max(JD) (or just existence of real eigenvalue 1).

### Zames-Falb / repeated nonlinearities (the most promising "beyond diagonal" route since tanh is the SAME odd function in every coordinate)
- Kulkarni & Safonov, "All multipliers for repeated monotone nonlinearities", IEEE TAC 47(7):1209-1212, 2002 (READ via DTIC report ADA399708 text): a bounded linear operator preserves positivity for ALL repeated monotone nonlinearities iff its kernel matrix is doubly hyperdominant (off-diagonals <= 0, diagonal >= sum of |off-diagonals| in each row AND column); for repeated ODD monotone: iff doubly dominant (no sign restriction). Claimed complete ("no larger class").
- D'Amato, Rotea, Megretski, Jonsson, "New results for analysis of systems with repeated nonlinearities", Automatica 37:739-747, 2001 (KNOWN): Hermitian subclass of the above.
- Willems & Brockett 1968 / Zames-Falb 1968 / O'Shea 1967: origin; survey Carrasco, Turner, Heath, "Zames-Falb multipliers for absolute stability: from O'Shea's contribution to convex searches", Eur. J. Control 28:1-19, 2016 (KNOWN, not re-read).
- Kharitenko, arXiv:2607.23599 (2026, ABS): OZF test is NOT necessary for slope-restricted Lur'e systems (disproves Carrasco's conjecture) via full-block multiplier. Su, Seiler, Carrasco, Khong arXiv:2112.07456 and Khong & Su arXiv:2009.13571 (ABS): discuss (non)necessity.
- Relevance: for x' = -x + J tanh(x), the loop is G(s) = J/(s+1) with a REPEATED odd slope-[0,1] nonlinearity. Static multiplier test at omega=0: find doubly dominant M with M(I - J) + (I - J)^T M > 0 (plus frequency-dependent conditions / Popov term). Diagonal M is the LDS test that fails at 1/sqrt2; off-diagonal doubly dominant M is NOT excluded by our obstruction. Caveat: doubly dominant means sum_j|m_ij| <= m_ii, so off-diagonal mass is l1-bounded; for dense iid J the gain over diagonal may be O(1) or may vanish as N -> inf — needs a numerical LMI/SDP test at moderate N (feasibility vs g). Dynamic (frequency-dependent) Zames-Falb multipliers M(jw) = M0 - H(jw) with ||h||_1-type doubly-dominant constraints are strictly more general.
